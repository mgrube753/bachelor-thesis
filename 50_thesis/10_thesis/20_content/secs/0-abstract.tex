\begin{abstract}
    This Bachelor's thesis investigates the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) for Automated Question Generation (AQG) regarding two aspects: the adherence to given instructional content and alignment with Bloom's revised Taxonomy. The work is structured via two primary research questions, which will be answered by going through two certain experiments. The methodology integrates both quantitative and qualitative evaluations. Quantitative assessment utilizes cosine similarity measures and LLM-based adherence scoring to assess semantic similarity between generated questions and desired source content. Qualitative analysis involves expert evaluation using structured rubrics that assess criteria including e.g.\ relevance, clarity, answerability, and especially correctness to the source content in the first experiment. In contrast to correctness, the second experiment systematically explores the relationship between question formats (Multiple-Choice vs. Open-Ended) and cognitive complexity levels across Bloom's Taxonomy, analyzing how LLMs behave when generating questions with varying instructional information. Results indicate a significant impact of prompt complexity, since vastly written prompts do not imply higher scoring of e.g.\ content fidelity. Even state-of-the-art LLMs struggle with this task, and generating questions for educational purposes -- including Bloom's revised Taxonomy -- remains a challenging task. Overall, this research demonstrates the promise and challenges of using LLMs for educational question generation and proposes a comprehensive evaluation that combines automated metrics with expert\,/\,student assessments to present future directions for improving AQG systems.
\end{abstract}
