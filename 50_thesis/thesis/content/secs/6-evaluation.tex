\subsection{First Experiment}
\label{sec:first-experiment}

The first experiment of the thesis focused on evaluating the content fidelity of questions generated by different \ac{llms} using two distinct prompt strategies: a common prompt and a complex prompt. In this experiment, the goal was to determine whether the complexity of the prompt influences the content fidelity and final scoring of the generated questions, addressed in the first research question:

\requ{1}{To what extent do \ac{llms} adhere to the content of diverse provided instructional texts when generating questions?}

\object{Quantitative Analysis} semantic sim via cossim vs. llm-based content adherence calculation, there are tables and plots needed.

\begin{itemize}
    \item question type was not fixed, but via format constraints limited to either Open-Ended or Multiple-Choice questions. trend towards mc questions, open ended is exp1a 9/168 (only complex prompt, 8x openai, 1x google), exp1b 4/56 questions (1x common prompt deepseek, 3x google). maybe also add here the table 1 from intermed
    \item hard to interpret these results without the qualitative analysis, since the qualitative will also provide sampled results for content adherence (1a) / manipulation handling (1b). without this, the llms, the input sources and the prompt types are hard to compare.
    \item 1a:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item at llms noticed: deepseek was noticably worse than the others in cossim, google best, anthropic pretty fine as well, openai in the middle / adherence score: all pretty high and similar
        \item at input sources noticed: the more complex the source, the lower the cossim (script > tanenbaum > cossim), but no real trend in adherence score
        \item comparison llm vs input source, in cossim still the trend of llms being best at script, medium at tanenbaum and worst at transcript, often noticably visible. adherence scores instead are mixed, but all of these are very high, > 90\%
        \item llms vs prompt types: at cossim common prompt commonly worse than complex prompt, but not always. adherence shows (pretty flipped) that common prompt results in higher scores than complex prompt, which is questionable.

        \item extra llm vs prompt type: common prompt same mean or higher than complex prompt, especially seen at anthropic and google / adherence shows that common prompt has way higher scores for each llm in contrast to complex prompt.

\subsection{Second Experiment}
\label{sec:second-experiment}

The second experiment addressed evaluating the relationship between question formats and Bloom's Taxonomy levels in \ac{llm}-generated questions. The goal of this was to determine how different question formats, Multiple-Choice and Open-Ended questions, perform across various cognitive levels of Bloom's revised Taxonomy. This experiment dealt with the second research question:

\requ{2}{How does the relationship between diverse question formats and Bloom's Taxonomy levels influence the pedagogical effectiveness of \ac{llm}-generated questions?}
